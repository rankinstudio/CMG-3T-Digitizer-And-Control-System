CMG-3T Digitizer

Three-component digitizer for a Güralp CMG-3T 120 s broadband (T35286): three ADS1220s for velocity, an ADS1115 for mass positions, on a Raspberry Pi. Two ElectroCookie solderable breadboards in a 3D-printed box. Drawings, tables and test steps; the software is in pi/ and pc/.

Sensitivity499 V/(m/s)at the ADC · 0.489 nm/s per count
Full scale±4.1 mm/ssensor clips at 13 mm/s
Build2 boards23 + 14 parts · 13 + 13 jumpers

Overview

The sensor already delivers flat velocity from 120 s to 50 Hz on low-impedance differential outputs, so there is no preamp: each component gets a matched divider onto a 1.668 V pedestal and its own ADS1220. All three converters share the Pi’s SPI bus.

Sensor cables

Two stranded Ethernet patch cables and a two-core power lead. At the sensor, each conductor ends in an insulated Dupont socket on its pin (letters on connector_pinface.png), with the housings retained and the bundle tied down to the floor before a loose loop. At the board box, each Ethernet cable plugs into a Cat6 keystone jack in the box’s front wall, punched down T568B, with short wires from the jack to the boards. One cable per board: SIG carries the three velocity pairs and signal ground, all to the ADC board; CTL carries the three mass positions and the control lines, all to the AUX board. Keep the three leads apart for the first half-metre from the sensor.

Why power has its own lead: plugging SIG and CTL into each other’s jacks is then harmless. The worst a swap does is put the lock line on signal ground and lock the masses. With 12 V in an Ethernet cable, a swap would drive the sensor’s outputs from the supply. Use two cable colours and two jack colours anyway, and label both ends. If shielded cable is used, the drain goes to GND at the board end only.

Each panel button shorts its line to Y; the optocoupler beside it does the same for the Pi. Solder the button lead into the same hole as the sensor wire. The lines are active-low (idle 5 V). The 12 V adapter enters through the VIN panel jack, wired to AUX A13 (+) and A14 (−); the sensor’s power lead leaves through the VOUT jack, wired to A17 (+) and A16 (−).

Assembly and test

Stage 1ADC board

  1. checkQuick meter check per module — The purple module ties AVDD to DVDD and pulls CLK to DGND with 10 kΩ (marked 103). Module unpowered: AVDD to DVDD ~0 Ω (Ω, 200 range), CLK to DGND ~10 kΩ (Ω, 20k range; not continuity, which can’t see 10 kΩ). It matters because AVDD gets no socket. A module that differs is a spare.
  2. Cut the female headers — Per module: one 8-pin (digital), one 6-pin and one 1-pin (analog). Cut through a contact, not between, and sand the ends.
  3. Jumpers 1–13 — All of them now, while the board is bare. Rail jumpers 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9 hop over the GND rail; keep them flat.
  4. Bus wires B1–B6 — SCLK, MOSI and MISO from module Z to N (row H) and N to E (row G), in the free holes between the socket rows. Insulated wire on the top side, lying flat: the modules sit over them on their sockets. Not the solder side. Fit before the modules, like Cdif. Then one plain Pi lead per signal is enough.
  5. Cdif ×3 under the modules — Lay each film cap flat with its body toward the channel, leads into E7/E8, E16/E17, E25/E26. This is the only chance to fit them.
  6. Sockets — Solder the three 8-pin strips in row I and the 1+6 strips in row D, with a module plugged in to hold them square. Then take the modules out again. No socket at D2, D11 or D20 (the AVDD pin): those columns carry the sensor’s minus lead, and a socket there ties it to 3.3 V. If one got fitted, clip the AVDD pin off the module instead; AVDD is fed through the module’s own AVDD–DVDD link.
  7. Dividers — Rbp flat in row B, Rb on end, Ra/Rap across the GND rail. Check each value with the meter before soldering: 4.99k and 10k 0.1% look alike.
  8. Reference — VR1 in H27–H29 (either way round), Rfd1, Rfd2, Cref, Cd, PJ.
  9. checkPower test, no modules, no sensor — First unpowered, Ω: A29 to VREF rail ~1.44 kΩ (2k range); 3V3 rail and VREF rail to GND not ~0 (200 range). Then Pi 3V3/5V/GND wires on, all readings on 20 V DC: 3V3 rail ~3.3 V; VREF rail = 2.50 V (the 20 V range shows 10 mV steps, the D-grade part is 1%, so anything 2.48–2.52 passes). With the sensor leads open, each AIN strip reads 2.50 V (no current in Rb). Short a sensor lead to GND and that AIN drops to 1.67 V.
  10. Modules in, Pi wires on — Nine plain leads: CS and DRDY for each module, and SCLK / MOSI / MISO into J5 / J6 / J7 only. Register readback on each module before anything else.
  11. checkShorted-input noise — Short each channel’s two sensor leads to GND and log ten minutes. Expect ~1.5 nm/s/√Hz equivalent at turbo 180 SPS (0.72 µV/√Hz), flat above 0.5 Hz; below that is what this test is for. Do this before the sensor is connected.

Stage 2AUX board

  1. Jumpers first, then the resistors — RmE I5→I1, RmZ I7→I12, RmN H6→H11 (flat); Rr upright from J5, J6, J7 across the GND rail to the VREF rail; Rk1–Rk3 in row H.
  2. Optocouplers — OK1–OK3 straddling the channel, pin-1 dot top right (in row F, on the right of each: F20, F23, F26). Rk1–Rk3 stand on end.
  3. ADS1115 — Check the chip marking first: BOGI is an ADS1115, BRPI an ADS1015 (reject). Solder the straight 10-pin header to the board (short pins into the board), then stand it upright in D3–D12, chip side toward row A, VDD at D3. Then jumpers 10–13: E9→F7 (A0, Z), E10→G6 (A1, N), E11→H5 (A2, E), E12→I4 (A3, VREF), insulated wire across the channel.
  4. 12 V island — D1 C13→C17, band toward 17; jumper B14→B16. Nothing here touches a rail.
  5. checkCheck before the sensor — Meter on DC V, 20 V. Adapter on A13/A14, Pi off: A17 reads ~11.6–12.3 V against A16 (12 V less the Schottky drop; unloaded adapters read high); Pi on, VREF wire in: i2cdetect -y 1 shows 0x48; with the mass inputs open, cols 7, 6, 5 (A0, A1, A2) read the same as VREF (~2.46 V; no current in Rm). Optocouplers on Ω, 2k, red probe on the collector: A20 to A19 reads open, then drops to a few hundred Ω while GPIO16 is driven high. Repeat A23 to A19 with GPIO20 and A26 to A19 with GPIO21.

Stage 3Box

  1. checkFit test — Print enclosure/fit_test.stl (outer face down, no supports): a keystone jack must click in from behind, a DC jack and a button must go through.
  2. Print the box — Base upright, open side up, no supports; lid flat face down. Walls 2.5 mm, honeycomb floor and lid.
  3. Front wall — Left to right: VIN jack (12 V adapter in, AUX A13/A14), AUX keystone (CTL), ADC keystone (SIG), VOUT jack (sensor power lead, AUX A17/A16), and the three buttons above. Keystones go in from inside, latch up. Label all four.
  4. Board stack — Four standoffs stand on the floor pads, studs down through the floor, nuts from below in the 10 mm pockets. The AUX board screws to them, the ADC board above it.
  5. Pi on the side wall — Four standoffs through the right wall from outside, nuts inside. Pi component side out, GPIO header at the top; its wires go over the top edge into the slot in the wall.
  6. Lid — Snaps on one way round, with its lip gap over the Pi slot. Six bumps click into the walls.

Stage 4Sensor

  1. stopHarness: buzz it out twice — Every conductor from its Dupont socket to its board hole, against the cable table, with the patch cables plugged into their jacks. The power lead gets checked alone: red on pin c, black on pin b. A wrong c/b pair is the one mistake that can hurt the sensor.
  2. Sensor locked, then connect — Plug in with the adapter unplugged. Adapter in: the mass positions read ±8 V on the AUX channels.
  3. checkOptocoupler drive, before any unlock — Sensor powered and locked. Meter on DC V, 20 V, black on A19 (Y): A20, A23, A26 idle at ~5 V. Test with LOCK only, since locking an already-locked sensor changes nothing: drive GPIO21 high for 1 s; A26 must fall below ~0.8 V. If it only sags, the LED current is too low for the sensor’s pull-up: parallel a second 1 k across each of Rk1–Rk3 (4 mA). Do not pulse CENTRE or UNLOCK here; the other two optocouplers are the same part and resistor.
  4. Unlock on its final pad — Level it with the feet and bubble, N mark to north. UNLOCK ~7 s from the button or cmgctl.py unlock: the motors run for a few minutes and the masses usually centre themselves (within ±2 V).
  5. Centre if needed — After ~4 hours of settling, any mass beyond ±2 V: CENTRE ~7 s (cmgctl.py centre), then wait two minutes before reading again.
  6. stopLock before moving — cmgctl.py lock, wait for quiet and all three masses at ~±8 V, then remove power. Never move it unlocked.

Everything is 3.3 V logic; the modules have no level shifters. SCLK, MOSI and MISO each run as one plain lead from the Pi to module Z; the board’s bus wires carry them to N and E. Nineteen Pi pins in all: 9 for the three converters, 2 for I²C, 3 for lock / unlock / centre, and power.

Notes

1

No series resistance in the sensor’s 12 V

The 3T runs off an internal switching supply, which draws more current as its voltage sags. A 10 Ω sense resistor in series runs away (180 mA and climbing). Only the diode sits in the line, and the adapter’s 1 A limit is the protection.

2

The 12 V return goes to pin b and nowhere else

The adapter− (A14) connects only to sensor pin b (A16). The sensor ties b to its signal ground internally, and signal ground N lands once, on the ADC board. So the 400–600 mA motor pulses never flow through either board’s ground.

3

Optocouplers keep the Pi off the sensor’s control lines

The Pi only lights an LED; the LTV-817’s transistor does the pulling, from the line to Y. The GPIOs default low at power-on, so a boot or a crash cannot lock or unlock the masses.

4

Why the AVDD socket is left out

Three modules take 24 of 30 columns, and each needs two strips of its own for the sensor leads. Leaving AVDD unsocketed frees its strip for the minus lead, and the minus-leg resistor lies flat over the four unused module strips. AVDD is still powered through the module’s own AVDD–DVDD tie, so meter that before building.

5

No Ccm capacitors

No 10 nF from each input to ground: the layout has no clean spot for six, and the 3T’s outputs are low-impedance, band-limited, and arrive over shielded cable. Cdif across each pair does the filtering the switched-capacitor input needs, since the PGA is bypassed.

6

PGA bypassed, gain 1

Both legs swing, so the common mode moves with the signal, and the PGA’s input limits would clip early. With the PGA out, only the pin limits apply: the inputs stay inside 0–3.3 V until ~6.7 mm/s, beyond the ±4.1 mm/s full scale.

7

Matched pairs are what reject the reference

VREF noise reaches both legs of a channel equally and cancels in the differential reading, to the extent the pairs match. That is what the 0.1% parts buy; the 1% mass-position dividers don’t need it.

8

Boards in a closed box, Pi outside it

Below ~0.05 Hz, the limit is the boards’ thermal drift, not the converter. The printed box keeps air movement off them, and the Pi, the main heat source, sits outside on the wall.

9

Pi 5: PCIe ASPM off

With ASPM on, the PCIe link to the RP1 (the chip behind the GPIO and SPI pins) drops into L1 between reads and puts an ~80 ms disturbance on all three channels every 2.77 s. Add pcie_aspm=off to /boot/firmware/cmdline.txt.
